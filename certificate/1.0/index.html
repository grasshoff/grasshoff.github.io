Famulus Certificate FC 1.0draft for discussion

Deed · 2026-11-21 · Deutsch

The Famulus Certificate states that a publication carries the justification of its claims in a form that others can examine: every claim as a proposition, every ground at a pinned address, the argument as an explicit structure, everything mechanical re-run by a checker that is not the producer's, and every judgement that no program settles listed and signed by a named person. It certifies a procedure and its record. It does not certify that a claim is true.

This is a human-readable summary of the specification. It is not a substitute for it. Specification.

Why this certificate

Reliable research

The certificate serves reliable research: a state of science of the highest quality at a time when the tools of artificial intelligence grow ever more powerful. Its object is the growth of knowledge — claims that a community can examine and accept for reasons — on a sound foundation in the theory of science.

Against a flood of unchecked texts

Generative systems write texts faster than any community can read them. Without standards, scientific information is flooded with unchecked texts, fabrications and unreliable output that meet none of the standards of scientific communication. A store full of such texts is an archive of claims, not a body of knowledge.

No way back

There is no way back to classical publishing. A text written by a human author alone, with at most a correction of its spelling, is unrealistic, and it is not desirable for method: the tools can reconstruct an argument, replay a computation and check a quotation at its source more completely than a reader can. What matters is not who typed a sentence but whether its justification can be examined and who answers for it.

Peer review, continued

Peer review reads a text and judges it; it cannot re-run every step. Automated scientific lectors that work under certified, openly stated standards take over the mechanical part of review completely: every ground at its place, every computation re-run, every argument made explicit. They leave to named referees, item by item, the judgements that no program settles. The thesis of this certificate is that in this division they fulfil the role of peer review better than a reading of the text alone. Famulus Lektor is the instrument that reconstructs the argument; the certificate continues its work into publication.

An open standard

The standards are put openly to discussion and then adopted with the broadest possible support (see "Open to discussion").

The foundation: computational epistemology

The certificate rests on the account of justification of computational epistemology (G. Graßhoff, Computational Epistemology: Foundations, Famulus Fundamentals No. 1, 2026). Its tenets, and the elements that implement them:

  1. A statement is justified relative to a declared space of evidence when it follows from addressed premises by the rules of the discipline and nothing in that space refutes it. Evidence justifies only as a premise of an argument. Not justified is not false: a missing ground justifies nothing and refutes nothing. → GR · AR · IC
  2. A compiled answer is a dependent witness. It restates its sources, adds no evidence, and its agreement with them confirms nothing — as a manuscript copied from an extant exemplar is no witness to the text. The output of a language model is therefore a reading to be checked, never a check. → IC · NV
  3. Where a question is open, the best explanation is the answer that survives the evidence discriminating between the candidates, not the answer that agrees with the most observations. → AR · RF
  4. A system that serves research is an instrument with which the scholar acts. It does not hold a proposition true and does not answer for one; the scholar who adopts its result asserts the proposition and answers for it. → AU · AC · CL
  5. A claim enters the body of knowledge of a community that includes generative agents when six conditions hold: truth, accountable assertion, novelty, best attestation, communicability and scrutiny. Truth cannot be checked from outside; the other five can be checked at publication, and the nine elements of the certificate measure them. → AU · CL · NV · GR · AR · IC · RF · EX · AC

What the mark says

Published according to the Famulus Certificate FC 1.0 [AU·CL·NV·GR·AR·IC·RF·EX·AC] — https://famulus.lettre.ai/certificate/1.0/

Before certification, the state is shown instead:

Famulus Certificate FC 1.0: pending — 3 of 9 elements hold [au·CL·nv·gr·ar·ic·rf·EX·AC] — https://famulus.lettre.ai/certificate/1.0/

What the mark does not say

It does not say that a claim is true, that the publication is important, or that its sources are complete. Truth cannot be checked from outside; the certificate secures the conditions under which others can examine a claim. A missing justification leaves a claim unjustified; it does not make it false.

The nine elements

A publication carries the mark only when all nine elements hold. There are no partial marks.

AU

Authorship

The design of the content, the argument, the thesis and the structure of the document come from the author; every draft text is edited by the author and answered for by him; Famulus has worked only under the editing rules of this certificate (rules B, S, T).

CL

Claim ledger

Every claim of the publication is stated as a proposition, with its place in the text, and marked as grounded, as a thesis, or as a gap.

CL1 checker · CL2 checker · CL3 author

NV

Novelty statement

The publication states what it adds, against prior work cited at its addresses.

NV1 checker · NV2 referee

GR

Pinned grounds

Every ground stands at an address that resolves at a fixed version, and the content cited there is identified by its hash.

GR1 checker · GR2 checker · GR3 checker · GR4 referee

AR

Explicit argument

The inference from grounds to claims is an explicit graph with typed edges; for every explanatory claim the rivals and the cases that discriminate between them are named.

AR1 checker · AR2 checker · AR3 checker · AR4 author

IC

Independent check

Everything mechanical is re-run by a checker written apart from the producer's software. Outputs of language models count as readings, never as checks.

IC1 checker · IC2 checker · IC3 checker · IC4 checker

RF

Referee sign-off

What no program settles is listed item by item, and a named referee other than the author signs each item.

RF1 checker · RF2 referee · RF3 checker · RF4 checker

EX

Readable exposition

A readable account links each claim to its place in the checked structure, in a page edition and a print edition with the same text.

EX1 checker · EX2 checker · EX3 series

AC

Accountability

A named author answers for the claims; contributions of AI systems are declared by part; the version is identified by content hashes; objections are recorded and answered.

Rules of examination, validation and editing

The certificate states that the design of the content, the argument, the thesis and the structure of a publication come from its author, and that every draft text was edited by the author, who answers for it. Famulus works on a text only under the following rules; every rule has a fixed id, and every finding under a rule is recorded with it (element AU). Rules of examination, validation and editing →

Who is trusted

Nothing that produces the publication is trusted — not the author's tools, not the agents or language models, not the publisher's services. Trusted are a small checker that anyone can run, written apart from the producer's software, and the decisions of named persons, each bound to the hash of the text it concerns.

How to mark a publication

Publish the certificate folder beside the version it certifies, check it with an independent checker, and print the mark the checker gives — in the page edition and in the print edition. Before every condition holds, show the state, never the mark.

Open to discussion

  1. Discussion. From 21 November 2026 the draft is open to anyone. A comment or objection names the condition it concerns (e.g. GR2) and gives its reasons; it is sent to the address below, by persons or by agents.
  2. Record. Every objection is recorded with its outcome and answered; one that convinces changes the draft. The record is public beside the specification.
  3. Adoption. After the discussion the version is adopted with the broadest possible support: persons and institutions that support it are named on its page. From then on it is in force, and it is never changed; a change is a new version.
