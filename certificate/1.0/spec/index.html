Famulus Certificate 1.0 — Specification

Format famulus-certificate/1 · version 1.0 · 21 November 2026 · status: draft for discussion (§11) · steward: OpenScienceTechnology GmbH (editor Gerd Graßhoff) · this text: CC BY 4.0 · address: https://famulus.lettre.ai/certificate/1.0/

This is the normative text. The deed (the short form for readers) and the machine-readable description (fc-1.0.jsonld) are written from the same list of elements and conditions, so that the three cannot disagree. Where they appear to differ, this text decides. The key words MUST, MUST NOT and MAY are used as in RFC 2119.

1. What the certificate states

A publication carries the Famulus Certificate when the justification of its claims is laid out in a form that others can examine and the examination is on record. The certificate is a set of files published with one version of the publication. Its state is computed from those files by a checker; nobody enters it by hand.

The certificate certifies a procedure and its record. It does not certify that a claim is true. Truth cannot be checked from outside; the certificate secures the conditions under which a community can examine a claim: an accountable assertion, the grounds at fixed addresses, the explicit argument, an independent re-run of everything mechanical, and a finite list of human judgements, each signed. A missing justification leaves a claim unjustified; it does not make the claim false.

The certificate is written for publications made with computer programs and language models, and applies to any publication. It is independent of the subject, the language and the publisher.

2. Why the certificate exists

Reliable research. The certificate serves reliable research: a state of science of the highest quality at a time when the tools of artificial intelligence grow ever more powerful. Its object is the growth of knowledge — claims that a community can examine and accept for reasons — on a sound foundation in the theory of science.

Against a flood of unchecked texts. Generative systems write texts faster than any community can read them. Without standards, scientific information is flooded with unchecked texts, fabrications and unreliable output that meet none of the standards of scientific communication. A store full of such texts is an archive of claims, not a body of knowledge.

No way back. There is no way back to classical publishing. A text written by a human author alone, with at most a correction of its spelling, is unrealistic, and it is not desirable for method: the tools can reconstruct an argument, replay a computation and check a quotation at its source more completely than a reader can. What matters is not who typed a sentence but whether its justification can be examined and who answers for it.

Peer review, continued. Peer review reads a text and judges it; it cannot re-run every step. Automated scientific lectors that work under certified, openly stated standards take over the mechanical part of review completely: every ground at its place, every computation re-run, every argument made explicit. They leave to named referees, item by item, the judgements that no program settles. The thesis of this certificate is that in this division they fulfil the role of peer review better than a reading of the text alone. Famulus Lektor is the instrument that reconstructs the argument; the certificate continues its work into publication.

An open standard. The standards are put openly to discussion and then adopted with the broadest possible support (see "Open to discussion").

3. The foundation: computational epistemology

The certificate rests on the account of justification of computational epistemology (G. Graßhoff, Computational Epistemology: Foundations, Famulus Fundamentals No. 1, 2026). Its tenets, with the elements that implement them:

  1. A statement is justified relative to a declared space of evidence when it follows from addressed premises by the rules of the discipline and nothing in that space refutes it. Evidence justifies only as a premise of an argument. Not justified is not false: a missing ground justifies nothing and refutes nothing. (→ GR, AR, IC)
  2. A compiled answer is a dependent witness. It restates its sources, adds no evidence, and its agreement with them confirms nothing — as a manuscript copied from an extant exemplar is no witness to the text. The output of a language model is therefore a reading to be checked, never a check. (→ IC, NV)
  3. Where a question is open, the best explanation is the answer that survives the evidence discriminating between the candidates, not the answer that agrees with the most observations. (→ AR, RF)
  4. A system that serves research is an instrument with which the scholar acts. It does not hold a proposition true and does not answer for one; the scholar who adopts its result asserts the proposition and answers for it. (→ AC, CL)
  5. A claim enters the body of knowledge of a community that includes generative agents when six conditions hold: truth, accountable assertion, novelty, best attestation, communicability and scrutiny. Truth cannot be checked from outside; the other five can be checked at publication, and the eight elements of the certificate measure them. (→ CL, NV, GR, AR, IC, RF, EX, AC)
condition of knowledge measured by the elements
Accountable assertion AC, CL
Novelty NV
Best attestation GR, AR, IC
Communicability CL, EX
Scrutiny RF, AC

Truth, the first condition, is not measured: it cannot be checked from outside.

4. Trust model

  • Untrusted: everything that produces the publication and its argument — the author's tools, agents, language models, the publisher's services and quality gates.
  • Trusted: (a) a checker that anyone can run, written apart from the producer's software, small enough to be read in full; (b) the decisions of named persons, each bound to the hash of the text and of the argument they decided on.

Whatever the checker cannot re-run MUST appear on the residual list (§8) and be signed. Nothing else is trusted.

5. Elements and conditions

The certificate has eight elements. An element holds when every one of its conditions has passed. A publication is certified when all eight elements hold. There are no partial marks.

Each condition names who settles it: the checker re-runs it from the certificate files; a gate of the publishing series measures it; the author decides it; a referee — a named person who is not an author — signs it.

CL — Claim ledger

Every claim of the publication is stated as a proposition, with its place in the text, and marked as grounded, as a thesis, or as a gap.

condition by requirement
CL1 checker The ledger lists at least one claim; every claim has an id, a text, a section of the publication and a status grounded | thesis | gap.
CL2 checker A grounded claim names at least one ground; a gap names what is missing.
CL3 author The author has agreed to the ledger for exactly this text.

NV — Novelty statement

The publication states what it adds, against prior work cited at its addresses.

condition by requirement
NV1 checker The certificate holds a novelty statement with at least one item; every item names the claims it concerns and at least one prior work by address or bibliographic key, or states that no prior work is known and where it was searched.
NV2 referee A referee has judged the novelty statement adequate.

GR — Pinned grounds

Every ground stands at an address that resolves at a fixed version, and the content cited there is identified by its hash.

condition by requirement
GR1 checker Every ground of the ledger is a Famulus reference (famulus-ref/2) pinned by @rel:, @snap: or @sha:.
GR2 checker For every ground the certificate holds the cited content; its SHA-256 under the declared normalisation equals the pin or the recorded hash.
GR3 checker Every quotation recorded as verbatim stands, under the declared normalisation, in the cited content of its ground; a quotation that is a rendering (a translation, a paraphrase in quotation marks) is recorded as such and appears on the residual list.
GR4 referee For every load-bearing premise a referee has confirmed an independent witness, or the premise is listed as resting on one witness.

AR — Explicit argument

The inference from grounds to claims is an explicit graph with typed edges; for every explanatory claim the rivals and the cases that discriminate between them are named.

condition by requirement
AR1 checker The certificate holds the argument graph; its hash equals argument_sha of the manifest.
AR2 checker Every edge has a kind from the declared list; every node an edge names exists; the support relation has no cycle.
AR3 checker Every claim marked explanatory names at least one rival and, for each rival, the discriminating cases at addresses, or states why the case that would discriminate is not available (the condition is then open); or it carries the gap 'no rival examined'.
AR4 author The author has agreed to every thesis, premise and step of the reconstructed argument for exactly this text.

IC — Independent check

Everything mechanical is re-run by a checker written apart from the producer's software. Outputs of language models count as readings, never as checks.

condition by requirement
IC1 checker Every file of the certificate has the hash the manifest names.
IC2 checker Every computation of the certificate recomputes from its inputs to its recorded output, or is listed as not re-runnable on the residual list.
IC3 checker Every output of a language model on which a verdict depends is recorded as a reading (model, instruction version, hashes of input and output) and appears on the residual list.
IC4 checker A checker run of this certificate, by a program that is not the producer's, reports every checker condition passed.

RF — Referee sign-off

What no program settles is listed item by item, and a named referee other than the author signs each item.

condition by requirement
RF1 checker The residual list holds, for every grounded claim, the item 'the ground carries the claim'; for every claim, 'the claim means what the text asserts'; for every reading, 'the reading is right'; for every explanatory claim, 'the rivals are adequate'; and the item 'the novelty statement is adequate'.
RF2 referee Every item of the residual list carries a signature: referee, date, decision (agree | disagree), reason; the referee is not an author.
RF3 checker Every signature is bound to the current text_sha and argument_sha; a signature to another hash does not count.
RF4 checker No item is signed 'disagree' unless the claim it concerns is withdrawn or marked as contested in this version.

EX — Readable exposition

A readable account links each claim to its place in the checked structure, in a page edition and a print edition with the same text.

condition by requirement
EX1 checker The page edition has an anchor for every claim of the ledger (#claim-<id>).
EX2 checker The page edition and the print edition are files of the manifest with their hashes, and the page edition carries the mark of this certificate with its state.
EX3 gate The print edition carries the same mark; the publishing series reads it from the rendered PDF.

AC — Accountability

A named author answers for the claims; contributions of AI systems are declared by part; the version is identified by content hashes; objections are recorded and answered.

condition by requirement
AC1 checker The manifest names at least one author with an identifier (ORCID or address) and the version with text_sha.
AC2 checker The AI declaration lists every part of the publication (text, preamble, argument, figures, computations, translations) with the systems that contributed and their role (none | assistive | drafting | evaluative); a part without an entry is a gap.
AC3 author The author has confirmed the AI declaration and released exactly this version (text_sha).
AC4 checker Every objection on record has a decided status, and every convincing objection is annotated in this version or an earlier one later than the version it read.

6. States

state rule
certified every condition of every element passed
pending no condition failed, at least one is open (a decision of the author or a referee outstanding, or a gap)
failed at least one condition failed

A condition is passed, failed or open. A gap is open, never passed. A service that does not answer is a gap.

7. Files

A certificate is a folder certificate/ published beside the editions of the version it certifies. All files are UTF-8 JSON. Paths are relative to the folder.

file content
manifest.json format (famulus-certificate/1), certificate (FC 1.0), specification (URL), publication {address, title, version, date, text_sha, argument_sha, authors [{name, orcid or address}]}, editions {page, print} (paths), files {path: SHA-256 of the bytes} for every other file of the certificate and both editions, normalisation (nfc-ws1), produced_by {name, version}, series_checks {condition: passed | failed | open} for conditions settled by a gate
claims.json list of claims: id, text, section, status (grounded | thesis | gap), grounds [pinned addresses], gap, explanatory (bool), rivals [{text, discriminating [addresses]}], rivals_gap, contested [objection ids], withdrawn
novelty.json {items [{text, claims [ids], prior [addresses or bibliography keys], searched}]} or {gap}
grounds.json list: address (pinned), base (the address without pin), layer, content (path of the cited content), sha256 (of the normalised content), gap
grounds/<n>.txt the cited content of a ground, as the resolver gave it at the time of the pin
quotations.json list: id, text, ground, section, kind (verbatim | rendering)
argument.json the argument graph: nodes [{id, …}], edges [{id, kind, head, tail [ids], …}]; argument_sha is the SHA-256 of this file
computations.json list: id, kind, inputs, program, output; kind arith with expression and decimals is re-run by every checker
readings.json list: id, model, instruction_version, input_sha, output_sha, verdict_of (what depends on it)
assents.json the author's decisions: author, text_sha, argument_sha, items, agreed, disagreed, open, ledger_agreed
residual.json the residual list (§8): id, kind, about, text
signatures.json list: item, referee {name, orcid}, date, decision (agree | disagree), reason, text_sha, argument_sha
declaration.json the AI declaration: parts [{part, systems [{name, role}], note}], confirmed_by, confirmed_on
critique.json objections: id, status, annotation {since_version, text}
release.json the author's release: by, on, version, text_sha, or {}

Edge kinds: deductive, defeasible, computational, explanatory, attack, summary. Parts of the AI declaration: text, preamble, argument, figures, computations, translations. Roles: none, assistive, drafting, evaluative.

Pins. A ground is a Famulus reference (famulus-ref/2) with a version selector: @rel:<tag> (a release), @snap:<n> (a snapshot) or @sha:<hex> (8 to 64 lower-case hex digits, a prefix of the SHA-256 of the normalised cited content). A short reference fr:<Sigle>[<Teil>] is expanded to its full address before it is pinned; the certificate holds only full addresses.

Normalisation nfc-ws1. Unicode NFC; every run of white space replaced by one space; leading and trailing space removed. Hashes of cited content and the search for quotations use it.

8. The residual list

The residual list holds the judgements no program settles. It MUST contain, at least:

kind about one item for
support <claim>, <ground> each ground of each grounded claim: the ground carries the claim
witness <claim> each grounded claim: an independent witness confirms the load-bearing premise, or it rests on one
meaning <claim> each claim: the claim means what the text asserts
rivals <claim> each explanatory claim: the rivals and discriminating cases are adequate
reading <reading id> each reading of a language model: the reading is right
rendering <quotation id> each quotation that is a rendering: it renders the source faithfully
computation <computation id> each computation the checker cannot re-run
novelty statement the novelty statement is adequate

A referee signs items, not the publication. A signature to another text_sha or argument_sha does not count. A referee who disagrees makes the item fail unless the claim is withdrawn or marked as contested in that version.

9. The checker

A conforming checker:

  1. imports no code of the producer and calls none of its services; it MAY ask a public resolver for the current content of a ground, and reports a difference as drift, not as failure;
  2. never calls a language model;
  3. reads only the certificate folder and the editions it names;
  4. reports each condition settled by the checker as passed, failed or open, and reads the records of the author, the referees and the series for the others;
  5. reports the state, the number of elements that hold and the mark (§10).

The reference implementation is fc_check.py (Python standard library only), published with this specification together with test vectors (vectors/): every implementation MUST reproduce the expected state of every vector.

10. The mark

A certified publication MUST carry the mark, in its page edition and its print edition:

Published according to the Famulus Certificate FC 1.0 [CL·NV·GR·AR·IC·RF·EX·AC] — https://famulus.lettre.ai/certificate/1.0/

A publication that is not certified MAY show its state, never the mark itself:

Famulus Certificate FC 1.0: pending — 3 of 8 elements hold [CL·nv·gr·ar·ic·rf·EX·AC]

In the state code an element that holds is written in capitals, an open one in lower case, a failed one struck through. The mark links to this specification; the page edition links the certificate folder. Machine-readable: the page head carries <link rel="famulus-certificate" href="certificate/manifest.json">, and the JSON-LD of the publication names the specification as usageInfo and the state as creativeWorkStatus of an item of hasPart with @type CreativeWork and name Famulus Certificate FC 1.0 (see fc-1.0.jsonld).

11. Discussion, adoption, versions

Status: draft for discussion.

  1. Discussion. From 21 November 2026 the draft is open to anyone. A comment or objection names the condition it concerns (e.g. GR2) and gives its reasons; it is sent to the address below, by persons or by agents.
  2. Record. Every objection is recorded with its outcome and answered; one that convinces changes the draft. The record is public beside the specification.
  3. Adoption. After the discussion the version is adopted with the broadest possible support: persons and institutions that support it are named on its page. From then on it is in force, and it is never changed; a change is a new version.

Address for comments and objections: gerd.grasshoff@opensciencetechnology.com. Record: discussion.html.

A published version of this specification is never changed once it is adopted. A change is a new version at a new address (/certificate/<version>/); what was certified under an earlier version stays certified under it. Each version lists its changes.

12. Relation to the Famulus Expertise series

Famulus Expertise requires the certificate for every issue (rule R11 of the series). The quality seal of the series is the certificate plus the publication conditions of the series (register, files, citation form, counting mark, licence) plus the author's release. The gates of the series that measure an element name its code.
